## Context

This project was carried out as part of the **Convex Optimization course at EFREI** and explores the application of machine learning to **intrusion detection in IoT environments**.

The study focuses on the **RT-IoT2022 dataset**, which contains more than
123,000 network-flow observations representing both legitimate IoT traffic and
multiple attack categories, including DDoS, network scans, ARP poisoning,
Slowloris, and SSH brute-force attacks.

Rather than focusing solely on classification accuracy, the project investigates
the trade-off between **predictive performance, computational cost,
interpretability, and mathematical structure**.

Two Support Vector Machine approaches were compared:

- a **Linear SVM**, directly connected to the convex optimization concepts of
  margin maximization, regularization, slack variables, duality, and KKT
  conditions;
- an **RBF Kernel SVM**, capable of modeling more complex non-linear decision
  boundaries through the kernel trick.
