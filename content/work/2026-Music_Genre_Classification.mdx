## Context

This project was developed as part of a **Neural Networks and Deep Learning**
course and explores **automatic music genre classification** at the intersection
of audio signal processing and deep learning.

The objective was to classify **30-second music clips into 8 different genres**
using the **Free Music Archive (FMA Small)** dataset, which contains 8,000
balanced tracks across Hip-Hop, Pop, Folk, Experimental, Rock, International,
Electronic, and Instrumental.

Rather than relying on a single modeling approach, the project follows a
progressive experimental pipeline, starting with classical acoustic features
and gradually moving toward deeper audio representations.

Several approaches were investigated:

- **Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and a Multi-Layer Perceptron** trained
  on handcrafted acoustic descriptors such as MFCCs, spectral features, chroma,
  RMS energy, and tempo;
- a **Convolutional Neural Network trained from scratch on mel-spectrograms**,
  allowing the model to learn both temporal and spectral patterns directly from
  audio representations;
- an improved CNN using **SpecAugment, Batch Normalization, Dropout, and L2
  regularization**;
- a **transfer learning approach using Google's VGGish model**, pre-trained on
  AudioSet, followed by a lightweight MLP classifier.
